MenuCaptain

Privacy Policy

Effective October 15, 2026

MenuCaptain is a private notebook for eating out: you log restaurants and visits, scan menus, remember the dishes you loved, and (if you want) get AI-powered calorie estimates. It is owned and operated by MilSpo Life LLC, a Texas limited liability company based in Dallas, Texas (“we,” “us”). This policy explains what information the service handles, why, and the choices you have. The short version: your food log is yours. We use your data to run the app for you — we do not sell it, and we do not show ads.

Information we collect

  • Account information. Your email address and a password. Passwords are stored only as secure cryptographic hashes by our authentication provider — we never see or store your plain password.
  • Your content. The restaurants, visits, dish ratings, notes, collections, scanned menus, and photos you add to your log. This content is private to your account.
  • Replies to friends. If you reply to something a friend shared with you (a one-tap reaction or a short message), we store it so that you and that friend can both see it. Either of you can delete the conversation at any time. The first reply in a conversation is also emailed to your friend as a notification.
  • Chat with us. If you write to us from Settings, we keep the conversation so we can answer you, and we are notified by email when you write. Either of us can delete it at any time.
  • AI usage records. When you use an AI feature we record that a call happened, its size (token counts), and its estimated cost. We use this to enforce fair usage limits and protect the service from abuse.
  • Technical logs. Standard server logs (such as request timestamps and status codes) used to keep the service healthy and debug problems.

We do not collect advertising identifiers, we do not use analytics trackers, and we do not buy data about you from anyone.

How we use your information

  • To provide the service: storing your log, syncing it across your devices, and showing it back to you.
  • To power the AI features you invoke (described below).
  • To enforce usage limits, prevent abuse, and keep costs sustainable.
  • To respond when you contact us for support.
  • To send you an occasional email about what’s new in MenuCaptain. Each one has a link to stop them, and stopping them doesn’t affect emails about your account or things friends send you.

AI features

Some features send data to Anthropic (the AI provider) for processing, only when you use them: scanning a menu sends your menu photos; estimating a dish from a photo sends that photo; text-based estimates and “Help me order” send the relevant dish names, menu text, and your question. The results come back to your log. Under Anthropic’s API terms, this data is not used to train their models by default.

If you leave the app while a menu is being read, our server keeps what was read from it (the text, not your photos) until you open it, and deletes it after 7 days at the latest.

Service providers

We use a small set of providers to run MenuCaptain. Each receives only what it needs to do its job:

  • Supabase — database, authentication, and photo storage (where your log and photos live).
  • Railway — hosts our backend server.
  • Anthropic — AI processing for the features above.
  • GitHub Pages — serves the app itself and any list pages you choose to publish.
  • Google — Google Places, for “Near me” discovery, looking a place up on Google, and showing a place’s rating and photos. The location, search terms, or place you request are sent to Google.
  • OpenStreetMap (Nominatim) — when you tap “Find address,” the restaurant name and city you typed are sent to look up its address and map location.
  • Serper — when you tap “See photos” on a dish, the dish name (and cuisine) is sent to find matching photos from the web.
  • Firecrawl — when you import a menu from a link, the page at that URL is fetched and rendered so the menu can be read.
  • Resend — sends account and notification emails (for example sign-in, password reset, and connection, share or reply notifications) and our occasional update emails to your email address.
  • Cloudflare — stores encrypted nightly backups of the service’s data and photos, so nothing is lost if our main storage fails.
  • Stripe — payment processing for paid plans. Stripe handles your card details directly; we never see your full card number.

Sharing and published lists

Nothing in your log is shared unless you share it. If you use the share or publish features, the specific content you choose becomes visible to anyone who has the link or image — treat any shared link as public. What travels depends on what you share:

  • A place or list — the restaurant name, address, cuisine, and (if you include it) its menu. A place can also carry your star rating for it and the names of the dishes you loved there. When you send a place to a friend inside the app, photos from your visits there go too unless you untick the box.
  • A visit — the place, the date, your star rating for the place, the names of the dishes you had (and whether you marked them as loved, would order again, or a special), the meal photos, the menu, the names of the people you tagged as being there, any note you write for the reader, and, unless you untick it, what you have recorded about the place itself. It does not include your private notes, your notes or ratings on individual dishes, or any amount you spent.
  • A collection — for each meal in it, the place and its city, the date, your star rating, the names of the dishes you had (and whether you marked them as loved, would order again, or a special), and, unless you untick the box, up to three photos a meal, plus any note you write for the reader. It does not include who you were with, your private notes, or any amount you spent.

Shared pages are hosted on our server (and shared list/menu pages on GitHub Pages) so the link works for whoever you send it to. Anyone the link is forwarded to can also open it, so only share what you’re comfortable being seen. Photos in something you share stay visible through that link for as long as you keep them in MenuCaptain; delete a photo and it stops showing there too. A friend who logs a visit you shared keeps their own copy of its photos.

When you send a place, a visit or a collection to a friend inside the app, we keep a short record of it: what it was, who you sent it to, and when. This fills your Sent list. It does not include the photos or the contents. You can remove an entry, and the record is deleted with either account.

Data storage and security

Your data is stored in our hosted database and photo storage, isolated per account. Connections are encrypted in transit (HTTPS), access to your data requires your authenticated session, and provider credentials are kept on our servers — never in the app on your device. The app keeps a copy of your session and a local cache on your device so it loads fast; signing out clears them.

Retention and deletion

We keep your content for as long as your account is active so your log is there when you come back. To delete your account, your log and your photos, use Delete account in Settings. You can also email support@menucaptain.com and we will remove it within 30 days.

Each night a copy of the service’s data and photos is saved with Cloudflare. Each night’s copy is kept for 30 days, so something you delete, including your whole account, is gone from the backups within 30 days. Short-lived data, like the text of a menu waiting to be opened after a scan, is never copied.

Children

MenuCaptain is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them.

Health disclaimer

Calorie and macro figures produced by the AI features are estimates for curiosity and memory. They are not medical, dietary, or nutritional advice and should not be relied on for health decisions.

Changes to this policy

If we make material changes, we will update this page and its effective date. Continued use of the service after a change means you accept the updated policy.
