# MenuCaptain - User Guide

*Updated July 2, 2026*

MenuCaptain is your private notebook for eating out. You log the places you go,
scan their menus, remember which dishes you loved, and - if you want - get a
rough idea of the calories on your plate. It is built to help you *remember and
enjoy* what you eat, not to police it.

Everything you save lives in your own private account, separate from everyone
else's. Your places, visits, photos, and menus are yours alone.

-----

## Getting set up

Create an account with your email address and a password - right from the
sign-in screen. You're in right away - no confirmation email to chase down. Because
your email is how you'd recover the account, sign-up asks you to type it twice
so a typo can't lock you out later. That is the whole setup.

If you ever forget your password, tap "Forgot password" on the sign-in screen
and the app emails you a reset link (so it's worth getting your email right at
sign-up). Signing in on a new phone or computer is just your email and password
- your data follows your account.

## Getting around

The app opens to **Home**, and there is a bar across the bottom with five spots.
The one you are on is highlighted.

- **Home** - your start screen, built around two doors. **Already at the
  restaurant?** is right on top: it's a two-step for when you've sat down. First
  it finds *where you are* (tap **See places near me** and it uses your location to list the
  spots right around you, or search / pick a saved place), then it walks you into
  **getting the menu** for that place - so the menu always lands on a real,
  Google-linked place instead of a name you typed. If a menu is already on file
  there, it offers to open it instead of re-scanning. If you have none but other
  diners have already added one, it offers theirs first - with how old each is -
  and **Get a fresh menu instead** right underneath if it looks out of date. That same step also lets you
  **log a visit** right away, to save the memory of the meal. The second door,
  **Looking for somewhere to eat?**, opens a find-a-place page: **Find a place by
  name** (search your own places or anywhere on Google) at the top - it's ready to
  type the moment it opens - and under **Not sure where to go?** the discovery
  helpers: **Near me** (what's around you), **Still to try** (saved places you
  haven't been to - mark one **Want to try** to pin it, or **Dismiss** it to
  clear it off the list), and **Can't decide?** (a pick from your saved places). Back on
  Home, below the two cards, are any active group order and a look at what you've
  been up to lately - **Recently added** (your newest saved places, each with the
  date you added it) and **Recent visits** (the meals you've logged). (Scanning is
  also on the **+** button and every place page.) **Settings** is behind the gear,
  top-right. The flow runs find a place &rarr; open it and scan the menu / ask
  Help me order &rarr; log the visit. **Looking somewhere else?** On the find-a-place
  page a **City (optional)** box appears once you start typing. Leave it blank and
  the search leans on where you are now; type a city (say, Austin) and it searches
  there instead - handy for a spot in another city you can only half-remember
  ("something with 'red' in Austin"). Each result shows its cuisine and address so
  you can spot the right one, and when you add a place its Google photo, rating,
  and hours come along automatically.
- **Places** - your list of restaurants.
- **The plus (+) button** - quick actions, not a page. **Already at the
  restaurant** comes first - the same two-step as the card on Home. **Find places
  near me** discovers spots around you, and **Find a place** searches your places or anywhere on
  Google. Below them are the things you do once you're there: scan or find a
  menu, log a visit, split the check, or start a group order. **Find friends**
  is here too, so your connections are always one tap away; if someone has
  asked to connect, a badge shows the count.
- **History** - everything you have logged, newest first.
- **Discover** - the home for Your Year in Food, your Food
  passport, dining companions, your published lists, your saved splits, and any
  active group orders.

**Settings** (the gear on Home) holds your account and plan, the look of the app
(five color themes under Appearance), the update check, this user guide, app
info, and which screen the app opens to (Startup).

## Places (your restaurants)

**Adding a place.** Add a restaurant with its name, cuisine, city, address,
phone, website, and any notes. You can mark it as a favorite and give it a
photo. Only the name is required - everything else is optional.

**Find address.** When adding or editing a place, tap **Find address** under
the address field and the app looks it up by name and city, fills in the
address, and remembers its location for the map. When the place is well listed
on the map, it also fills in any details it can find - phone, website, cuisine,
and hours - but only for fields you have left blank, so it never overwrites
what you typed. It finds chains and well-mapped spots reliably; for a brand-new
or tiny place it may come up empty (or with just the address), in which case
just fill in the rest yourself.

**The restaurant page.** Opening a restaurant shows its details, its menus,
every visit you have logged there, and a picks card once you have rated some
dishes (see "Your picks" below).

## Map

Tap **Map** at the top of Places to see your restaurants as pins on a map.
Favorites show in the accent color, everything else in a softer tone. Tap a
pin to see the name and a button to open that restaurant.

The first time you open the map, any place that has an address but no pin yet
is located automatically, one at a time - you will see them appear over a few
seconds, with a "Locating your places" note at the bottom. Each place is only
located once and then remembered, so it is instant after that. If you change a
place's address later, the map quietly re-locates it.

If the active city filter is set on the Places tab, the map shows just that
city. Places with no address (or one that could not be found) are not pinned;
the bottom of the map tells you how many are not mapped.

## Near me

**Near me** helps you discover restaurants, cafes, bars, and bakeries close to
wherever you are, and add the good ones to your places in a tap. Everyone gets a
handful of free searches to try it; beyond that it is a **Pro** feature - open it
from the **+** button and choose **Find places near me**.

The first time, your phone asks to share your location; allow it and the app
finds what is nearby. Set how far to look (from half a mile up to 25 miles),
choose what you are after (all food, or just restaurants, cafes, bars, or
bakeries), and flip **Open now** if you only want places open right now. Tap
**Search nearby** and the results come back as a list, nearest first - each with
its rating, price, distance, and whether it is open.

**Open, closed, and closed for a while.** Anywhere the app lists a place from
Google it now says how it is trading. **Closed** just means shut at this hour -
worth ignoring if you are planning Saturday, which is why a closed place is
never hidden from a list. **Temporarily closed** is different and is shown in
red: the restaurant itself has stopped for a stretch, so it is not somewhere to
plan around. Open and closed are worked out from the restaurant's own posted
hours in its own local time, so they stay right no matter how long ago the
place's details were fetched. The one place a closed spot is held back is
**Can't decide?**, because that one picks for you.

Tap any place to see its details - address, hours, phone, website, a link to
Google Maps, and a short blurb. From there, **Add to my places** saves it
straight into your restaurants, already filled in (name, cuisine, address,
phone, website, hours) and pinned on your map, so there is nothing to type. If a
place is already in your list it is marked **In your places**, and tapping it
opens the one you already have.

Switch between **List** and **Map** any time - the map shows the results as
orange pins with a blue dot for where you are; tap a pin to open that place. The
list is sorted nearest-first, and the List/Map toggle is instant. Changing the
distance or type starts a fresh search (tap **Search** again).

**Can't decide? Pick for me.** When the list of nearby spots leaves you stuck,
tap **Can't decide? Pick for me** at the top and the app chooses one for you,
with a one-line reason. It weighs your taste - the cuisines and dishes you tend
to love across all your places - against each spot's rating and how close it is.
In a brand-new area where it does not know your taste yet, it leans on rating and
distance and says so. Want to steer it? Tap a **vibe** - Something light, Quick
bite, Date night, Cheap eats, Treat myself, or Try something new - and it picks
with that in mind. Not feeling the pick? Tap **Pick again**. (This is the
nearby-spots version of the "Can't decide?" spinner further down. That spinner
can build its wheel from your SAVED places - or, with "Discover nearby" on,
from spots around you, which uses the same Near me discovery allowance.)

Near me is the one place in the app that reaches out to find somewhere new -
everything else stays your own private record.

## Menus

**Scanning a menu.** Use "Scan or find a menu" and take or pick photos of the
physical menu. The app reads it and turns it into a clean, digital menu -
sections, dish names, descriptions, prices, and tags. This is what powers
"Help me order" and the calorie estimates. Prefer not to photograph it? **Find
the menu for me** looks on the restaurant's website and reads the menu in for
you - and if no website is on file, you can simply type it in. You can also
paste a link straight to a menu page.

**Fixing a scanned dish name.** If the scan misread a dish or two, open the menu
and tap **Edit names & tags** - each dish name becomes editable, so you can fix a
typo in place. Changes save as you go, and each dish keeps its price and tags.

**More than one menu.** A place can have several menus (for example Dinner,
Brunch, and Drinks). When you scan a menu that has more than one section, the
app asks how to save it: **One menu** keeps every section together in a single
menu, or **Customize each section** lets you split the sections into separate
menus (any sections you give the same name merge back together). So a single
scan of a long menu can be saved as separate Dinner, Brunch, and Drinks menus.

**Where menus show.** On a place's page, each menu appears as a card in the
**Menus** section - tap a card to open the full menu, with Help me order,
Explain the menu, and the calorie tools inside. With several menus, press and
hold a card near the grip to drag it into the order you like.

**Dietary filters.** Open a menu and tap the diet chips (vegetarian, vegan,
gluten-free, and friends) to narrow the list to dishes that fit - tags come
from the menu itself plus the scan's best read of each dish.

**Re-scanning and deleting.** Each menu card offers **Refresh** to replace it
with a fresh version - photograph it again or pull it from a link - and you
can delete a menu you no longer want from inside it. Refreshing replaces the
old copy in place (no duplicate), so any estimates saved on the old items are
not carried over.

**Adding a menu that already exists.** If you go in through **Get the menu**
rather than Refresh and give it a name the place already uses, the review screen
says so and asks which you meant: **Replace it** (the usual answer - a matching
name almost always means a fresher copy of the same menu) or **Keep both**.

**Calories on a menu.** If the menu itself prints calories, the app captures
those and shows them as a real number labeled "per menu." If it does not, each
item offers a few ways to estimate, best first: **Add your photo** (snap the
real plate right from the menu), **Estimate from menu photo** (the restaurant's
own picture, when the menu came in from the web), and **Estimate from
description**. You can redo an estimate from a better source any time. Whichever
you use, it is only an estimate, always shown with a "~" and marked EST, so a
printed calorie count stays the most reliable number.

**See what a dish looks like.** Under each menu item, the photo button says what
you will get. **See your photo** shows the picture you took of that dish here.
**See the restaurant's photo** shows the one that came with their online menu.
When there is neither, it reads **See similar on the web**: that searches
the web for photos of a dish with that name, so you can get an idea before you
order. Those are look-alikes, not this restaurant's version, and they are labeled
that way. When a real photo exists, it is the only one shown - no guesses on top.

**Pull a menu from a link.** You do not have to scan a paper menu. When adding a
menu you can paste the address of the restaurant's online menu and the app reads
it into a clean digital menu for you. If you are not sure of the link, tap **Find
the menu for me** and the app looks on the restaurant's website for it. This
works on regular web menus, on the big online-ordering pages (Toast, Square, and
similar), and on **PDF menus** that have real text in them - including sites
(common on Squarespace and Wix) whose menu page just links to a menu PDF: the app
follows through to the PDF and reads it. If the site has **several menus** (say
dinner, drinks, and dessert), the app lists them so you can add each one. A menu
that is just a scanned image has no text to read, so for those it is best to scan
it with the camera instead.

**Sharing a place or its menus.** Every way to share lives under one **Share**
button on the place page. Tap it and pick:
- **Send to a friend** - drop the place and its menu into a friend's "Shared
  with you" inbox (in-app, for people you're connected with).
- **Share a link** - a link anyone can open, no account needed.
- **Add to the community** - contribute your menu(s) so other diners at
  this place can skip scanning. You choose which menus to share; only the menu is
  shared, never your visits, notes, or ratings.

Separately, when a place is Google-linked, a **Menus shared by other diners**
panel lets you *pull down* a menu someone else already digitized here. You can
share any time (not only right after scanning), and the app gives a gentle
**"Share your menu?"** nudge so it's easy to find. Community menus you pull are
labeled "not verified." Tip: rename a menu to what it really is (say **Lunch**
instead of the default Main) *before* sharing; if you rename and re-share later,
the app tidies up your old copy so there's no stale duplicate.

## What only the locals know

Two kinds of thing a menu will not tell you, and both live where you need them.

**Signature dishes.** A menu lists forty things and does not say which one
people actually come for. On any menu, tap **Edit names & tags** and you can
mark a dish as a **Signature**. It then shows a star and a line under the dish
name wherever that menu appears, so the thing this place is known for is obvious
at the moment you are choosing rather than after you have ordered.

**Off the menu.** The drink the regulars ask for by name. The dish they will
make if you know to ask. On a restaurant page, under the menus, there is a
**Not on the menu** card - tap **+ Add**, give it a name, and optionally a price
and a note on how to ask for it or why it matters.

These live on the *place*, not on one menu, because a bar with a food menu, a
brunch menu and a drinks list has one set of things that are on none of them.
They also join the dish suggestions when you log a visit, so once you have
learned about something you never have to type its name from memory again.

Each one is dated and carries a **Still true** link. Tap it when you confirm the
thing still exists; after about six months without confirmation the app says so.
This matters more here than anywhere else in the app - a tip that has gone stale
sends somebody to ask a bartender, out loud, for something that no longer
exists.

## Help me order

On any digitized menu you can ask a plain-English question - things like
"something healthy," "not too heavy," "best value," "what did I love here?," or
"what do I usually get?" The app answers using that restaurant's actual menu, and
it draws on your own history there - which dishes you marked Loved, Again, or Skip, how
often you've ordered them, and what you've paid - so the more you log, the more
personal the answers get. When a menu item has calories on it - whether printed
on the menu or estimated - the answers factor those in too, so a question like
"something lighter" can weigh the actual numbers. Prices work the same way: ask
for "best value" or "something cheap" and it weighs what dishes cost, leaning on
the real prices it has learned from your scanned checks. It stays easygoing about
it: the numbers inform the suggestion, they are never treated as a budget or a rule.

It also keeps a light read on your broader taste - the kinds of dishes you have
loved (and the ones you have skipped) across all your restaurants - and uses it
as a gentle hint. That is most helpful at a place you have barely been to, where
there is little history yet, so even a first visit gets a personal suggestion. It
never overrides what you actually asked for. When you are done with an answer, tap
the small x in its corner to close it and clear the space.

## Marking a dish from the menu

You do not have to open the visit form to record what you ordered. On any menu,
under each dish, there is **I had this**.

Tap it and you get an optional comment box and three buttons: **I ordered this**,
**Have again**, and **Not again**. No photo is needed, and none is asked for -
this is for the very common case where you ordered something, had a view about
it, and either forgot to take a picture or had already started eating.

The dish goes onto today's visit at that restaurant. If you have not logged
anything there today, one is started for you with just that dish on it - your
rating and notes stay empty, so the record says only what is actually known. Tap
it again later and it edits what is there rather than listing the dish twice;
the row shows **Had it today** with your comment so you can see what you have
already marked without leaving the menu.

**Have again** and **Not again** are worth the extra tap. That verdict is what
"Help me order" and "You usually get" are both built from, so recording it at
the table is worth far more than trying to remember it later.

## Logging a visit

Tap the plus button and choose "Log a visit." You can fill in as much or as
little as you like:

**Date and rating.** Pick the date and give the visit a star rating. You can
rate in half-stars: tap the left side of a star for a half, the right side for
a whole. Tap the rating you already have to clear it. You rate each visit, not
the restaurant - so a place can have a different rating every time you go. On a
restaurant, in the Places list, and on a shared card, the star number shown is
the average of your visits there, labelled "avg."

**What you had (dishes).** There are three easy ways to add what you ordered, top
to bottom. **Search or type** in the box to find any dish on the menu (or type a
new one). **Your usuals here** is a row of one-tap chips - the dishes you have
ordered at this place before, your favorites first - so a repeat visit is a couple
of taps with no hunting. And the full menu is there too: if it is long it tucks
behind a **Browse the full menu** button so it does not fill the screen; tap to
open it and tap any item to add it. Adding a dish by tapping it from the menu is
the better habit - it makes the rest of the features line up correctly (see the
photo estimate below).

**Something not on the menu.** A special, an off-menu ask, a dish the scan
missed - type it and the list offers **Add "whatever you typed"**. It goes on
the visit like any other dish.

**Two dishes with the same name.** Some places list one dish under several
headings - a pancake house might have the same berry special as a pancake, a
waffle, french toast and a crepe. Those are different dishes, so the app keeps
all of them and tags each with its section, like *Signature Berry Bliss (Belgian
Waffles)*, so your log records the one you actually ate.

**Loved, Again, or Skip.** Each dish has three buttons. Tap **Loved** for a
standout you adored, **Again** for something solid you would order again, or
**Skip** for something you would not. This is the heart of the app - it is how
you remember what is worth getting next time. The most recent verdict always
wins, so you can change your mind later.

**Rename a dish.** Tap any dish's name to rename it - useful when a
free-typed name needs tidying up. The dish keeps its price, verdict, and any
estimate. This works while logging and later, from any past visit.

**Add a note to a dish.** Each dish has an **Add a note** button for the little
details worth remembering - a substitution or a special request, like "added
mushrooms," "no onions," or "well done." Your note shows under the dish and is
saved with the visit, so when you look back later you see exactly how you had it.

**Scan the check.** When the bill arrives, tap "Scan the check" (under the
dish list) and snap a photo of the receipt. The app reads it and opens a
"Which were yours?" editor: each line has a round checkbox - tap the ones you
ordered (or use Select all / Clear), and tap Done to add them as dishes with
their prices. When a line has more than one of an item, a small - / + stepper
lets you say how many of them were yours ("2 of 3"). When a line matches the
restaurant's digitized menu, the dish takes the menu's proper name, with a
small note showing how it appeared on the check. The editor works both ways:
after a scan the button reads "Edit dishes from the check", and re-opening it
shows your current picks already ticked, so you can add or remove items later
without paying for another scan. (The scan buttons themselves read "Scan or
upload itemized check" and "Scan or upload final / total receipt"; once the
total is captured, that section folds away.) The receipt itself stays attached to the visit behind
a quiet "Show receipt" link - it never appears in your photo strip or Food
passport.

**Final receipt (for expenses).** The check you scan is the *itemized* one. The
*final* receipt - the payment slip with tip and total - is what an expense report
needs, so there's a separate **Add final receipt** slot. Scan it or upload a photo
(the picker offers both), and when you create an expense item from the visit, the
**Share** attaches that final receipt automatically.

**Shared a dish?** If you split something - half a sea bass, a third of a
seafood tower - tap "Was this shared?" under that line in the picker and set
your share. It starts at 50 percent, with quick 1/2, 1/3, and 1/4 taps, or
type any percent. Your price becomes just your share of the line (30 percent of
a $44 fish is $13.20), and the dish shows the percent beside its name. If you
estimate that dish's calories from the menu description, the number is scaled
to your share too; a photo of your own plate is already your portion, so it is
left as is.

**Prices.** A dish can carry a price - typed in or picked up from a scanned
check. Priced dishes show it beside the name in History and on the
restaurant page.

**Spending.** Once your visits carry prices, a quiet **Spending** link appears
on a restaurant's page and inside Your Year in Food. Tap it to see what you
have spent there (or that year) - it stays tucked away by default. When a
visit has a scanned check, the total is the real one, tax and tip included;
otherwise it is a "~" estimate added up from your dish prices. Like the
calorie numbers, it is a memory, never a budget.

**Estimate a dish from a photo.** Under each dish is an "Estimate from a photo"
button. When the food arrives, tap it and snap a picture of that dish. The app
estimates the calories and macros for it. If that dish is on the restaurant's
digitized menu, the estimate (and a thumbnail of your photo) is saved onto the
menu item too - so the next time you open that menu, the picture and the
numbers are already there. If that menu item already had an estimate, the app
asks whether to keep the old one or use your new photo. Estimating works best
when you added the dish by tapping it from the menu, so the names match.

**Estimate without a photo.** Did not snap a picture? Each dish also has an
"Estimate without a photo" option that estimates from the dish name - grounded
by the menu description when you tapped the dish from the menu, so it is a
better guess than the name alone. Tap "Redo" on any estimate to clear it and
choose either method again.

**Running total.** Once you have estimated a dish, a calm total appears in the
visit ("~610 cal total - 31P / 63C / 22F across 2 dishes") so you can watch it
add up as you log - a number for memory, never a budget.

**Estimate the whole plate.** If your meal is not on a menu, there is also a
"Estimate from a photo" option for the whole plate, which gives one set of
numbers for the meal. You can edit any estimate by hand if you know the real
figures.

**Photos and notes.** Attach photos of the visit and jot down any notes.

**Which photo leads.** The first photo is the **Cover** - the one that heads the
visit, and the one somebody sees first when you share it. Every other photo has
a **Make cover** button, so putting the right one in front is a single tap. This
works on a visit you saved months ago, not just a new one.

**Photos on a single dish.** Separate from the visit photos, each dish has its
own **Add a photo** - up to three, purely as pictures, with no estimate
involved. Use it for the plate itself when the visit photos are of the table and
the people. If you *do* estimate calories from a photo, that picture is kept on
the dish too rather than being used and discarded.

## History

History is the running list of everywhere you have eaten, newest first. Each
entry shows the rating, the dishes (color-coded by your Loved/Again/Skip verdict,
with the estimated calories beside each one when you have them), any nutrition
summary, your notes, and your photos. When you have estimated two or more
dishes on a visit, the entry also shows a calm running total (for example
"~1,240 cal total across 3 dishes") - a number for memory, never a budget.

## Searching

As your lists grow you can filter them quickly:

- On **History**, the search box at the top filters your visits by restaurant
  name, by dish, or by anything in your notes. A **Sort** row lets you order them
  by newest, oldest, highest rated, or restaurant - and the sort and search work
  together.
- On **Places**, the search box filters your restaurants by name, cuisine, or
  city, and works alongside the city filter chips. A **Sort** row lets you order
  them alphabetically (A-Z), by recently added, or favorites first - and the
  sort and search both work within whichever city you have selected.

Type to filter instantly; tap the (x) to clear it and see the whole list again.

## Your picks (what you loved)

Once you have rated a few dishes at a place, its restaurant page shows a
picks card with two parts:

- **You've loved these here** - the dishes you have marked Again, so you can
  reorder your favorites without thinking.
- **Last time** - a quick reminder of your most recent visit.

Marking a dish Skip just keeps it off the loved list. Not ordering something
again does not count against it.

## Your Year in Food

Open **Your Year in Food** from the **Discover** tab, or from
**Settings** - either way you get a recap built entirely from what you have
logged - a calm look back rather than a scoreboard.

At the top you can switch between any year you have visits in, plus **All
time**; it opens on your most-logged year so there is always something to see.
The recap can include:

- how many visits you logged, and how many different places they were
- new spots you discovered that year
- the cities you ate in
- your #1 spot - where you went the most
- your top cuisine
- your most-loved dish, with the other dishes you marked Again
- your highest-rated visit
- your busiest month

Each card only appears when there is something to show, so a quiet year stays
short. There is no calorie total here, on purpose - in keeping with the spirit
of the app, the recap is about places, dishes, and memories, not a number to
measure yourself against.

You can share the recap as an image: tap the **share** icon at the top and the
app renders the year you are viewing onto a card you can text or post.

-----

## Your food passport

**Food passport** is a photo gallery of everywhere you have eaten - open it from
the **Discover** tab, or from **Settings**. Every photo you have
added, whether to a whole visit or to a single dish, shows up as a tile, grouped
by month with the newest first. Tap any photo to jump straight to that visit.

Think of it as the picture-first companion to History: History is the detailed
log, the passport is the gallery. The first time you open it, photos that are
not cached yet load over a few seconds, and are quick to view after that.

## Your badges

Open the **Discover** tab to find **Your badges** - a small set of stamps the
app awards as you use it. They are milestones, not a scoreboard: there is nothing
to chase and nothing you can lose. A badge you have earned shows in full color
with a note of what you did; one you have not earned yet sits quietly greyed
out with a hint of how to get it. Next to each badge is a quiet button that
opens the thing it is about - tap **Add a menu** beside First Menu, **Publish a
list** beside First List, and so on - so a badge doubles as a shortcut, never a
nag.

The starting set:

- **First Menu** - save your first menu, scanned or found.
- **Ten Visits** - log ten visits.
- **Five Cities** - eat in five different cities.
- **First List** - publish a list.
- **Five Shares** - share the app five times (see below).

When you earn one, a brief note slides in to mark the moment. Anything you
earned before badges arrived simply shows as already earned, without a fuss.

## Friends

MenuCaptain has a light social layer built around your **@handle** (set it in
Settings, under Your profile). Open **Friends** - at the top of the
**Discover** tab, or from the **+** button - to find friends by their handle
and send or accept requests. Once connected you can send a place, a menu, or a
visit straight to a friend from any Share sheet, and anything they send you
appears under **Shared with you**, with a banner on Home so you never miss it.

**If someone is a problem.** Every person and every shared item has a &#8942;
button. **Report** tells us what is wrong and a person reads it. **Block** is
private: they are never told, they are removed from your friends, they cannot
send you requests, visits or menus, and anything they share with other diners
stays hidden from you. Undo it any time under Settings, Blocked people. Community
menus also hide themselves once several people report them.

**Getting paid back.** Add **Your Venmo** to your profile and your friends'
apps learn it automatically, so when someone splits a check with you they can
send your share in one tap - no more asking for handles. Only people whose
requests you have accepted can see it, and you can clear it any time.

**Call people what you call them.** Usernames are rarely how you think of
someone. On any friend, tap **+ Add your name for them** and type the name you
know them by, so "@ashuttles" simply reads "Ashley Williams". It is your own
address book: their account and handle never change, and nobody else sees it.
The name follows them everywhere - your friends list, the people you tag on
visits (including visits you logged before the rename), split-the-check
suggestions, group-order invites, and Venmo requests. Clear it and they go
back to their own name.

**Sharing a visit.** Right after you save a new visit, the **Visit logged** message
at the bottom has a **Share it** button - the quickest way. Later, open a logged
visit and tap **Share your visit**. One
sheet, two ways: **Send to a friend** puts it in another MenuCaptain user's
"Shared with you" inbox, and **Share a link with anyone** makes a page that
works for people who have never heard of the app. Either way it carries the
place, your star rating alongside Google's, what you had, the photos, who you
were with, and the menu. It never carries your private notes or what you spent,
and the dish list is names only - no per-dish notes and no prices you paid.

**Say something about it.** The share sheet has a note field, and whatever you
write sits at the top of the page they open - "loved going to dinner after mass
with the family". It is the one part written for the reader rather than for you,
so it leads. This is not the private note on the visit; that one never leaves
your phone.

**They can connect back to you.** A link you share carries your name (and your
@handle behind it), so somebody who reads it and makes an account is offered you
as a friend in one tap. Set a display name under Settings &rarr; Your profile and
the page reads "Chris Gramlich" instead of "@cjgramlich" - including the line
under your stars, which otherwise has to say "the sharer". If the visit is one you were both at, their request also books their place
at it: the moment you accept, the visit appears in their diary with the date,
the people, and your photos. Until you accept they see "Waiting on you" and
nothing happens - being friends is what stops a forwarded link from letting a
stranger write into your visit.

**Two ratings, clearly labelled.** Your stars rate the PLACE, not that one meal,
so the page says "their rating of this place" and shows Google's score beside it
with its review count. Nobody has to guess whose opinion they are looking at. If
a place has no Google score saved, sharing fetches it once.

**A meal you were both at.** Share a visit with someone who was actually there
and they get an extra choice: **I was there**. That joins your visit rather than
starting a separate one. The place, the date, and the photos are shared from
then on, so a photo either of you adds turns up on both phones, and both visits
read "Shared visit with ..." so you can see at a glance who you were with. What
you ate, your notes, and what you spent stay yours alone, and your ratings stay
separate - you are welcome to disagree about the food. If you were not there, **Want to
try** still saves it as a recommendation instead.

**Who was with you?** When logging a visit, tag the people at the table. Those
names build your **dining companions** view in the Discover tab - who you eat
with most, and every meal you've shared. Tagging a connection links the visit
to their handle.

**Explain the menu.** On any digitized menu, tap **Explain the menu** and pick
the dishes you want decoded - the AI explains unfamiliar dishes and terms in
plain language, using what is actually on the menu.

## Sharing a menu

Once a restaurant has a digitized menu, you can send that menu to someone who
does not have MenuCaptain at all - handy for the "I am at the restaurant, what
do you want me to bring home?" moment.

Open the menu and tap the **share** icon in the row next to re-scan and delete.
(You reach that row by tapping a menu, or by tapping **Manage** on a place that
has just one menu - and on a single-menu place there is also a quick **Share**
button right beside **Manage**.) The app builds a small public page for the menu
and hands the link to your share sheet, so you can text it like any other link.

Whoever you send it to opens the menu right in their browser - no app, no
account, no access to anything else of yours. They see the restaurant's menu:
the sections, the dishes, descriptions, prices, and tags. They can **tap the
dishes they would like**, which gather into a "your picks" strip at the bottom.
Each dish they pick gets a small **note** field too, so they can add special
instructions ("no onions," "dressing on the side"). They then tap **Send my
picks** to fire their own share sheet with the list ready to text back to you,
each note in parentheses - "Avocado Toast (no onions)". No account, just a quick
answer back.

The shared page is only the menu - your own private calorie estimates are never
part of it. As with a published list, treat the link as public: anyone who has
it can open that menu page.

## Can't decide? (spin to pick where to eat)

Stuck on where to eat? From Home, tap **Looking for somewhere to eat?** and
choose **Can't decide?** Pick
up to eight places for the wheel - your favorites are ticked to start, and you
can search to add or remove any others - then tap **Spin**. The wheel is a
ship's helm: tap **Spin** to set it turning, then tap **Stop** whenever you're
ready and it coasts to rest on one of your places, dimming the rest. You get its
name with quick buttons to open the place, get directions, or spin again. Pick
fewer than eight and some spokes become "Spin again" - land on one and just spin
once more. Nothing is saved - it is just a fun way to settle the
"I don't care, you pick" stalemate.

## Group order

When you are out with a group, a **group order** is the live version of "what
do you want me to bring home?" - instead of everyone texting you separately,
they all add to one shared order you watch come together.

You can start a group order three ways: from the **restaurant page** (tap
**Group order**), from the **+** button (**Start a group order**), or from any
open menu (tap **Group order** at the top). If the place has more than one menu,
you pick which menus to include - all of them by default - so your group can
order across several at once (say pasta and pizza in one list); a place with a
single menu jumps straight in.

**No menu? Order free-form.** A group order doesn't need a menu. When you start
one, choose **Just a quick order** to name the place, or on the "Which menus?"
screen tap **start a no-menu order** - and for a saved place with no menu
scanned yet, it offers this automatically. Enter the restaurant name, an
optional **note to everyone** (deadlines, details - it appears at the top when
they open the link), and, if you like, a list of **options they can pick from**
(one per line - think a catering run: Ham/Cheddar, Turkey/Swiss...). Share the
link: with options, everyone taps their choice (quantity and a note work as
usual) and can still **write in their own words** below; without options, they
just type their order in a single **Your order** box. Same live tally on your
side, no menu needed.

Tap **Start group
order** and the app gives you a link. Send it to your group. Each person opens
it (no app, no account), sees the menu, taps what they want, sets a **quantity**
and an optional **note** ("no onions"), enters their name, and taps **Send my
order**. They can change their order and send again any time.

**Not hungry? Anyone can pass.** Right under the order button there's a **"Not
eating? Tap to pass"** option, so someone who isn't ordering can bow out in one
tap instead of leaving you wondering. They add their name so you know who it is,
and it's reversible - an **"Actually, I'm in"** button brings them back if they
change their mind.

On your screen the order builds **live** - the people who have ordered show up
first under **Who's ordered**, with a combined bring-home tally below ("2x
Avocado Toast, 1x Pollo - no onions"). Need to fix an order? Tap **Edit** on their card to change quantities or items,
or **Remove** to take them off - the tally updates. Step away any time with
**Leave open** (the order keeps collecting and waits under Active group orders);
tap **Close order** only when you are done - it stops new picks and also closes
itself after a day. The shared link shows only the menu and the
table's own picks - nothing else of yours.

**Knowing who you are still waiting on.** Optionally, add the people you are
expecting under **Who's in on the order?** on your host screen. As orders arrive,
each name is marked **ordered**, **passed**, or **waiting**, with a clear "Still
waiting on: Molly" line until everyone is in. Someone who taps **pass** counts as
responded and drops off the waiting list (shown as **passed - not ordering**), so
you are never left waiting on a person who was never going to order. Matching is
by name, so it lines up when a person types the same name you listed; anyone who
orders under a name not on your list still shows up under **Also ordered**, so no
order is ever missed. The roster is yours alone - guests never see it. Leave it
empty and the order works exactly as before.

**Getting back to a running order.** Any group order you start also appears under
**Active group orders** at the top of your **You** tab, with how many people
have ordered and when it expires - tap it to jump straight back into the live
view. Because that list lives with your account, you can start an order on one
device and pick it up on another.

**Splitting the check.** Open **Split the check** on your host screen to see who
owes what. Tap **Scan the receipt** and the app reads the real total, assigns
each line to whoever ordered it, and spreads tax and tip across everyone - so
the per-person amounts add up to the actual check. Anything it can't match to a
person is split evenly and labeled. Before the bill arrives it shows an estimate
from menu prices (before tax), and you can switch to an even split any time.

## Where shall we eat?

The other half of eating together: deciding. Open **Find a place** (from the
**+** button, or **Looking for somewhere to eat?** on Home) and tap **Eating with
people?**. Pick two to five of your places, and send the link to whoever is
coming.

It is an **approval vote**, not a poll. Everyone ticks *every* place they would
be happy to go to, not just their favourite, so the winner is the place most
people can live with rather than the one that scraped a plurality while half the
group quietly hated it. Nobody needs the app or an account to vote.

**Nobody sees the tally while it runs**, on purpose - if people could watch it,
late voters would follow the leader and one early tick would steer dinner. The
result appears when the vote closes, which happens on its own after the time you
set, or when you tap to close it early.

**Fair to everyone, not convenient for you.** If you are not all starting from
the same place, you can enter everyone's postcodes and the app will suggest
places that split the travel, rather than the ones nearest whoever happened to
be organising.

If somebody votes under a name you do not recognise, the app asks you once who
it was and remembers the answer. It never guesses - two people called Mark at
one table is ordinary, and a wrong guess would quietly file somebody's dinner
under the wrong person for good.

## Splitting a check (no group needed)

Sometimes everyone's at the table and you just want to split the bill - no group
order required. Tap the **+** button and choose **Split a check**.

Scan the receipt (or tap **Enter by hand**) and the app reads the line items and
the total. Type in who's at the table, then tap the names under each item to say
who shared it - anything you leave unassigned is split evenly across everyone.
There are **Tax** and **Tip** fields - each can be a dollar amount or a percent -
and the **Total** updates as you go, or you can type your own to hard-code it. An
**Everyone shares everything** shortcut for the simplest case. When a line covers
several of the same item (like **3 GL Prisoner** at one price), a **Split into 3**
button breaks it into separate per-unit rows so you can give each one to a
different person.

Tap **See the split summary** and you get each person's total - their items plus a
proportional share of tax and tip - adding up exactly to the real check. **Share**
hands a plain-text breakdown to your phone so you can drop it in the group chat.

**Save split** keeps it: give the split a name and it lands under **You ->
Your splits**, where you can tap it to reopen and edit it later, or delete it.
Saved splits are their own thing - they live separately from your visits and
don't touch your History. (Don't need to keep it? Just **Share** or tap **Done**
and nothing is saved.)

You can also attach a split to a visit you've already logged: open the
visit (in History or on the restaurant page) and tap **Split the check**. It opens
already filled in with that visit's dishes and total, and a **Show receipt** link
lets you check the prices against the actual check. Add who was there, tap **Save
split**, then **Save to this visit** - it is stored right on that visit (you'll
see a **Check split** line in History) without touching the rest of the meal.

You can also split while you are logging: under the scanned receipt in **Log a
visit**, tap **Split the check**, build the split, and tap **Use this split** - it
rides along on the visit and is saved when you save the visit.

## Publishing a list
On the **Discover** tab under **Your lists**, tap **Publish a list**, give it a title,
and tick the restaurants to include (your favorites and
highest-rated are listed first). Tap **Create link**, then **Copy** or **Share**.

When you publish, the app creates a small public page for the list and gives
you a short link to it - and when you text that link, it shows a proper
preview card with your list's name instead of a random web address. The first
time a particular list is published, its page can take up to a minute to go
live; anyone who taps right away sees a brief "almost ready" note that opens
the list automatically. Sharing the same list again later is instant.

If the app cannot create the short page, it simply shares the full-length link
instead, which always works; a **Shorten** button appears for those long links
if you want a tidier one.

**One-tap city share.** If you have a city selected in the Places filter, a
**Share my [city] spots** button appears. Tap it and the app builds a list of
just that city's places and opens your share sheet straight away - no titling or
ticking needed.

**My lists.** Once you have published at least one list, a **My lists** entry
appears under **Your lists** on the **Discover** tab, next to **Publish a list**. It keeps every list you
have published so you can re-share or copy its link, **favorite** one to pin it
to the top, sort them (recent, newest, A-Z, or most places), and **refresh** a
list to rebuild it from your current places - handy when you have added or
renamed spots since you first shared it (refreshing can produce a new link).
Deleting a list here just removes your saved copy; the public page stays up, so
links you already sent keep working.

The whole list is public once shared, so whoever you send it to just taps it
and sees your picks - each place with its city, cuisine, your rating, the
dishes you loved there, and the address with a **Directions** link that opens
their maps app. They do not need the app, an account, or any access to your
private data. Treat anything you publish as public: the link can be opened by
anyone who has it, the published page is hosted publicly, and it includes the
street address of each place you share.

The shared view is text-only - no photos or private notes - so it stays light
and loads instantly.

## Understanding the calorie labels

A few small labels show up wherever calories appear:

- **~320 cal** - the "~" means this is an estimate.
- **850 cal, per menu** - a real number printed on the menu (no "~").
- **EST** - short for estimated.
- **9P / 22C / 22F** - grams of protein, carbs, and fat.

A small tag also shows where a number came from. From most reliable to least:

- **per menu** - the real number printed on the menu. Always the most trustworthy.
- **your photo** - estimated from a picture you took of the actual plate.
- **menu photo** - estimated from the restaurant's own photo of the dish (the one
  that came in when you pulled the menu from the web).
- **description** - estimated from the dish description alone, no picture. The
  roughest guess.
- **edited** - a number you typed in by hand.

That order is why, when a dish has a real photo, the app leans on it before the
description, and why you can always redo an estimate from a better source.

## Your plan and the AI features

The AI features - menu scanning, "Help me order," and the calorie and macro
estimates - are built in. There is nothing to configure and no key to manage.

Every account starts on the **free plan**, which includes a taste of the AI
features so you can try everything. The Plan card in **Settings** shows how
many AI uses you have left. **Pro** removes the everyday limit, with a
generous monthly allowance that normal use will not reach - scan menus and
estimate plates freely.

**Have a code?** If you were given a friends & family code, enter it on the
Plan card in Settings ("Have a code?") and your account flips to Pro through
the date shown - no payment needed.

Upgrading, switching plans, and canceling all happen from the Plan card in
Settings. Payments are handled securely by Stripe; the app never sees your
card details. If you cancel, Pro keeps working until the end of the period you
paid for - the card shows the exact date.

Everything that is not AI - logging visits, rating dishes, places, the map,
History, Year in Food, the passport, and sharing - is free and unlimited on
every plan.

## Exporting your history

You can take your whole dining log with you. In **Settings**, under **Your
data**, tap **Export your history** for two ways to get it out:

- **Spreadsheet (CSV)** - every visit and dish in full detail: date, place,
  city, cuisine, your rating, each dish with its Loved/Again/Skip verdict, price, and
  any calories and macros. It opens in Excel, Numbers, or Google Sheets. On a
  phone, your share sheet pops up so you can save it to Files or email it to
  yourself; on a computer it downloads.
- **Printable** - a clean page grouped by visit, newest first. Tap **Print /
  Save PDF** to save it as a PDF or print it on paper - a nice keepsake.

Photos are not included in either one - both are text only. The export is built
right on your device from what you have already logged.

## Your data and privacy

Your logs, menus, and photos are stored privately in your MenuCaptain account.
They are not shared with anyone else, they are never used for advertising, and
nobody else can see them - sharing only ever happens when you explicitly share
or publish something yourself.

Your password is never stored in readable form, and signing in on a new device
needs only your email and password - your data follows your account, not your
phone.

Questions or trouble? Email **support@menucaptain.com**.

## If something stops working

- **You can't sign in.** Tap "Forgot password" on the sign-in screen and use
  the reset link the app emails you. If the email doesn't arrive within a
  minute or two, check your spam folder - and note the app sends at most one
  email per minute to the same address, so give it a moment before retrying.
- **The password-reset email never arrived.** Wait a minute and check your spam
  folder. If it still doesn't come, the email on the account may have a typo from
  sign-up - reach out at the support address and we'll help sort it out.
- **An AI feature says you've reached your limit.** Open the Plan card in
  Settings - free accounts include a set number of AI uses, and upgrading to Pro removes the everyday limit. Everything that is not AI keeps working
  regardless.
- **A photo is slow to appear or won't load.** Photos load from your account
  the first time you view them, so the very first view can be slow; it is
  quick after that. If one refuses to load, it may not have finished
  uploading - try again on a good connection.
- **Your data looks out of date on this device.** Open Settings and tap
  "Sync now." If something still looks wrong, sign out and back in - your
  data lives in your account, so nothing is lost.
- **A dish photo estimate didn't show up on the menu.** The dish name has to
  match a menu item exactly. Adding the dish by tapping it from the menu
  guarantees the match; a free-typed name that doesn't match will still get an
  estimate on the visit, it just won't attach to the menu item.

## The spirit of the app

The calorie and macro numbers are here for curiosity and memory - a record of
what you ate, not a budget to stay under. The app will never scold you, warn
you, or tell you that you "blew" anything. If the numbers ever start to feel
like pressure rather than interest, ignore them - the dish ratings and the
memories are the real point.
